Flotilla 1.5.0-beta.6 · public beta

Test the beta

This test is for people who have never used Flotilla, on a Mac that has never had it. It starts from nothing: downloading, installing and your first container. Each step says what to do and what you should see. Mark it Pass, Fail or Skip as you go; a Fail opens a GitHub issue with the step already filled in.

You don’t need to know anything about containers. What confuses you is as useful to us as what breaks.

Core path, under 30 minutes

28 steps, from the download to your first container and a check for updates, with a few short first-impressions boxes. If you only have half an hour, do these.

Deeper path, optional

177 more steps in short sections. Do any of them, in any order, and skip what you can’t try.

Saved in this browser

Your marks and notes stay in this browser on this device, so you can close the page and come back. Nothing is sent anywhere until you submit an issue yourself.

Before you start

Be signed in to GitHub in this browser before you start. A free account is enough. Each Fail opens a prefilled GitHub issue, and you submit it yourself.

  • A Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) and macOS 26 or later, that has never had Flotilla on it.
  • About 30 minutes, a few GB of free disk space, and the Mac’s administrator password.
  • You don’t need to install anything first. The core path downloads Flotilla, and Flotilla offers to install Apple’s container for you, through Apple’s own Installer.
  • A Mac where a test app doesn’t matter. Flotilla never removes anything you didn’t ask it to, but this is a beta.
  • What to include in a report, and what not to, is in BETA-TESTING.md.

Your setup (goes into each report)

A few words you’ll meet

container
Apple’s free tool for running Linux containers on a Mac. You normally type its commands in Terminal; Flotilla gives it windows and buttons.
Container
A program, such as a web server or a database, packaged with everything it needs and running in its own small Linux virtual machine.
Image
The package a container is made from. Downloading one is called pulling it.
Volume
Storage a container keeps its data in, which stays when the container is deleted.
Suggestions
Ready-made recipes in Flotilla for containers and other things worth trying.
Widget
A tile on the Overview that shows one thing, such as your containers or your Macs. You choose which ones, and where they go.
Tasks
The panel where every job you start, such as a download, shows its progress.
Admin and host
An admin Mac looks after containers on itself and on other Macs, called hosts. With one Mac, it is the admin.
Flotilla Helper
A small background part of Flotilla, approved once in System Settings, for the few jobs that need administrator rights.

How reporting works

Flotilla’s issues are on GitHub, and you file them with your own GitHub account (a free one is fine). This page can’t file anything by itself. When you press Fail, it opens GitHub’s New issue page in a new tab with the step, what should have happened and your setup already filled in. You write what actually happened, attach a screenshot if you can, and press Create.

At the end, Send summary opens one more issue with your totals, the steps that failed and your first impressions.

Never paste passwords, tokens, private keys or personal data into an issue, a screenshot or a log. Issues are public. A support bundle (Help ▸ Create Support Bundle… in Flotilla) shows you every file before you save it: read it before you attach it.

This browser isn’t letting the page save anything (a private window can do that), so your marks and notes will be lost when you close it. Reports still work.

Get Flotilla

About 5 min · 3 steps

Flotilla is free. It comes from its GitHub release page, signed by its developer and checked by Apple.

  1. 1 Find the download

    Do Open the release page and scroll down to Assets.

    Expect The release is marked Pre-release. Under Assets are files named Flotilla-1.5.0-beta.6 ending in .dmg, .pkg, .zip and .tar.gz. Download the .dmg (a disk image), unless you know you want the .pkg (an installer package).

  2. 2 Install it

    Do Open the file you downloaded from your Downloads folder. For the .dmg: drag the Flotilla icon onto the Applications folder in the window that opens. For the .pkg: follow Apple’s Installer.

    Expect The disk image opens a window with Flotilla and a shortcut to Applications, and copying takes a few seconds. The package opens Apple’s Installer, asks for your password, and ends with “The installation was successful.” Either way, Flotilla is now in your Applications folder.

  3. 3 Open it for the first time

    Do Open your Applications folder and double-click Flotilla.

    Expect macOS asks its usual question for an app from the internet: “Flotilla” is an app downloaded from the Internet. Are you sure you want to open it? Press Open. (After the package, it may open straight away without asking.) There is no warning about an “unidentified developer”, an app that “can’t be opened” or “is damaged”, or Apple being unable to check it.

Was it clear which file to download and what to do with it? Did anything macOS said worry you? A sentence or two is plenty; leave it empty if nothing comes to mind. It goes into your summary.

The Setup Assistant

About 7 min · 8 steps

The first time Flotilla opens, a short guide called the Setup Assistant asks a few questions. Two words it uses: an admin Mac is one you use to look after containers, on itself and on other Macs; a host is a Mac that an admin Mac looks after. With one Mac, choose Admin.

  1. 4 Welcome

    Do Read the first page, choose Admin, and press Continue.

    Expect A window titled “Welcome to Flotilla”, with “Step 1 of 5” and the question “How will you use this Mac?”. Admin and Host each say in a line or two what they mean, and a note says you can change this later in Settings.

  2. 5 Make It Yours

    Do Choose an appearance (Auto follows your Mac’s light or dark mode) and a look, then press Continue.

    Expect A small sketch of the window changes as you choose, so you can see the result before you go on.

  3. 6 Meet Greta

    Do Read the page about Greta, decide whether to turn her on, and press Continue (or Not now).

    Expect Greta is Flotilla’s assistant. The page says she runs on this Mac with Apple’s on-device model, sends nothing away, and never runs anything herself. If Apple’s model isn’t available on this Mac, it says so and offers Not now.

  4. 7 Prepare This Mac: nothing happens without you

    Do Read this page before clicking anything.

    Expect An item called “Apple’s container”. If container isn’t on this Mac, a checkbox reads “Download and install container 1.5.0 when I continue”, with a line saying it is Apple’s installer, about 118 MB, that Flotilla checks it is Apple’s, and that Apple’s Installer asks for your password. If it is already installed, it says “Installed. Flotilla runs your containers with it.” Nothing has been downloaded yet.

  5. 8 Install Apple’s container

    Do If the checkbox is there, tick it and press Continue. Skip this step if container was already installed.

    Expect Flotilla shows each stage, such as “Downloading container 1.5.0…” and “Checking it is Apple’s…”. Then Apple’s Installer opens and asks for your password: finish it there. Flotilla carries on by itself when the Installer is done.

  6. 9 Online Checks

    Do Read the page, leave both boxes ticked for this test, and press Continue.

    Expect It says Flotilla goes online by itself only for these, and each is separate: “Check for Flotilla updates automatically” and “Check daily for new suggestions”, both ticked, each naming the websites it contacts.

  7. 10 You’re set up

    Do Look over the summary and press Start Using Flotilla.

    Expect A page titled “You’re Set Up” lists your choices (role, appearance, container, Greta, online checks) and says you can change any of it later in Settings. Then Flotilla’s main window opens.

  8. 11 Approve the Flotilla Helper

    Do If macOS tells you Flotilla added a background item, or Flotilla asks you to switch it on, open System Settings ▸ General ▸ Login Items & Extensions and switch Flotilla on.

    Expect The Helper is a small background part of Flotilla for the few jobs that need administrator rights, such as installing container. macOS asks about it once. Afterwards, the ● Helper dot at the bottom of Flotilla’s window is green, and hovering it shows “Flotilla Helper — Running” with a version.

Did you understand each choice, and Admin versus Host? Was it clear what container is and why it was needed? A sentence or two is plenty; leave it empty if nothing comes to mind. It goes into your summary.

A first look round

About 4 min · 4 steps

The main window has a sidebar of sections on the left, the section you chose on the right, a search box in the bar at the top, and a status bar along the bottom.

  1. 12 Overview

    Do Look at the page that opens first.

    Expect Overview is selected in the sidebar. Across the top is a band that says “Nothing needs attention” (or shows a notice), with an All Notifications link. Under it, a Get started card offers Run a Container…, Pull Image…, Add Host… and Try a Suggested Stack…. Under that are tiles called widgets, such as Fleet at a glance, Hosts and Containers, with counts that are zero or close to it on a new Mac. If container still isn’t installed, a band at the top says so, with a button to install it.

  2. 13 The status bar

    Do Look along the bottom of the window. Click ● Container.

    Expect On the left: ● Container, ● Helper, the version, “v1.5.0-beta.6”, and then when Flotilla last refreshed, such as “Updated just now”. With more than one Mac added, that line also says how many are connected, such as “2 of 3 Macs connected”. On the right: Tasks. Clicking ● Container opens a menu whose first line is “Running”. If it says Stopped instead, choose Start Container System and wait for the dot to turn green.

  3. 14 Note your version

    Do Open Settings (Flotilla menu ▸ Settings…, or ⌘,) and choose About. Copy the version line into “Flotilla version and build” at the top of this page, so your reports carry it.

    Expect “Version 1.5.0-beta.6 (a build number) · Tested with container 1.5.0”.

  4. 15 Find your way

    Do Click Containers, Images and Volumes in the sidebar, then Overview again.

    Expect Each opens on the right. An empty list says there is nothing there yet and offers a way to start. Looking changes nothing on your Mac.

From this window, could you tell what Flotilla is for? What would you have clicked first? A sentence or two is plenty; leave it empty if nothing comes to mind. It goes into your summary.

Your first container

About 9 min · 9 steps

A container is a program packaged with everything it needs, made from an image (the package) and run in its own small Linux virtual machine. Suggestions are ready-made recipes for ones worth trying. You’ll run Mailpit, a small tool developers use to catch test emails. It has a web page, needs no password, and is harmless to delete.

  1. 16 Open Suggestions

    Do Click Suggestions in the sidebar, just under Overview.

    Expect A row of tabs: All, Apps, Stacks, Volumes, Networks, Domains, Machines and Clusters, each with a count. The list is on the left, and a line under it reads “N suggestions · library” and a version.

  2. 17 Find Mailpit

    Do Open the Apps tab, type mailpit in the search box at the top of the window, and select “Mailpit Email Testing Tool”.

    Expect Its details appear on the right of the list: what it is, its image (ghcr.io/axllent/mailpit), its ports and when it was tested. Nothing has been created. (If Mailpit isn’t listed, Flotilla hasn’t fetched its suggestions yet: click the version in the status bar, choose Check Now under Suggestions library, and search again.)

  3. 18 Use it

    Do Press ▶ at the top of the details (its tooltip says “Use this suggestion…”).

    Expect The Run Container form opens across the window, already filled in: the image, the name mailpit, ports such as 127.0.0.1:8025:8025 (a different first number if 8025 is taken on this Mac), and a volume for its data. A preview shows the container run command it will use. There are no red errors. Nothing runs until you press ▶ on this form.

  4. 19 Run it

    Do Hover the ▶ at the top right of the form (tooltip “Run”), then press it.

    Expect You are back on Containers at once. A Tasks panel rises on the right with one line for the job and its progress: Flotilla downloads the image first, which can take a minute. Then mailpit is in the list and running, and the panel tucks itself away.

  5. 20 Open its web page

    Do In your web browser, go to http://localhost:8025 (use the first port number from the form if it was different).

    Expect Mailpit’s page, with an empty inbox.

  6. 21 Its page

    Do Select mailpit in the Containers list.

    Expect Its page opens to the right of the list, starting on Overview, with its state, image and ports. Along the top is a row of tab icons, each with a tooltip when you hover: Overview, Processes, Logs, Inspect, Terminal and Files.

  7. 22 Its logs

    Do Open the Logs tab.

    Expect The lines Mailpit printed as it started, including one saying where its web page is.

  8. 23 Stop it

    Do In the page’s header, press Stop (a filled square; its tooltip says “Stop mailpit”).

    Expect Within a few seconds it shows as stopped, in the list and on its page, and its web page no longer loads.

  9. 24 Delete it

    Do In the page’s header, press the trash can (tooltip “Delete mailpit”).

    Expect Flotilla asks first: “Delete “mailpit”?”, “This cannot be undone.”, with Delete and Cancel. After Delete, it is gone from the list. The volume made for its data stays in Volumes until you delete it there.

Was it clear that Use only opened a form, and nothing ran until you pressed ▶? Did the Tasks panel make sense? Was anything hard to find? A sentence or two is plenty; leave it empty if nothing comes to mind. It goes into your summary.

Download an image

About 2 min · 2 steps

Downloading an image without running it is called pulling it. alpine is a very small Linux image.

  1. 25 Pull an image

    Do Click Images in the sidebar and choose Pull Image… (also in the File menu). Type alpine:latest in Reference and press ▶ (tooltip “Pull”).

    Expect You are back on the Images list at once, and the Tasks panel shows the pull with a percentage. The window stays usable while it runs.

  2. 26 It arrives

    Do Wait for the pull to finish.

    Expect alpine is in the Images list, beside Mailpit’s image. About two seconds after the pull ends, the panel tucks away, and the status bar shows “Tasks · Done” for a moment, then how many jobs finished.

  2. 28 Check for updates

The search box

About 6 min · 8 steps

The box in the bar at the top of the window filters the list you are looking at, finds things by name, and runs commands. ⌘K puts you in it from anywhere.

  1. 29 ⌘K opens it

    Do Press ⌘K on any page.

    Expect The search box takes focus at once and lists commands, then sections.

  2. 30 It filters the page you are on

    Do On Containers or Images, type part of a name.

    Expect The list filters as you type, and the dropdown says “N on this page”.

  3. 31 Look at one kind of thing

    Do Type image: and then part of an image’s name. Try Tab while a kind is suggested.

    Expect Only images are listed, and the page behind is not filtered. Tab accepts the suggested kind.

  4. 32 Other kinds

    Do Try volume:, net:, suggestion: and host:.

    Expect Each lists only that kind of thing.

  5. 33 Commands by name

    Do Type /run-container, then /settings, then /greta.

    Expect Each command is listed, and Return or Tab runs it.

  6. 34 Commands only

    Do Type >.

    Expect Only commands are listed, and the page is not filtered.

  7. 35 Esc in the box doesn’t close a form

    Do Open a form (Pull Image…, say), type in a field, press ⌘K, then Esc.

    Expect Esc closes the dropdown. The form stays open with what you typed.

  8. 36 Search Docker Hub

    Do In Images, choose Browse Docker Hub…, type a name in the box and press Return.

    Expect Results from Docker Hub, a public catalogue of images.

Lists and pages

About 10 min · 12 steps

Every list in Flotilla works the same way, like Mail: rows on the left, the selected row’s page on the right. Running a few containers first (from Suggestions) gives you more to look at.

  1. 37 Rows everywhere

    Do Look at the list in each of Containers, Machines, Images, Volumes, Networks, Hosts, Registries, DNS and Kubernetes.

    Expect Each is a list of rows with a checkbox at the start of each row and a Sort menu.

  2. 38 The bar above a list

    Do Open Containers and look at the bar above the list, then at the checkboxes at the start of the rows.

    Expect The first thing in the bar is a checkbox, directly above the rows’ own checkboxes. Next to it are two buttons on one rounded bar, one with lines (list only) and one with two panes. The one in use is lit. After them come the Sort menu and, when there is something to choose between, Filter.

    Do Tick the checkbox in the bar, then untick it. Hover each of the two buttons.

    Expect Every row is ticked, then none. The tooltips say “Show List Only” and “Show Two Panes”.

  3. 39 The divider is remembered

    Do Drag the divider between the list and the page, switch to another section, then come back.

    Expect The width is remembered for each section. A section you have never changed starts with the divider in the middle.

  4. 40 Rows fit their width

    Do Make the list wide, then narrow.

    Expect Rows become one line when there is room, and two or three when narrow. The small labels (chips) drop before the name is ever cut short.

  5. 41 Row actions on hover

    Do Hover a row, then select one. Open the ⋮ menu at its end, then right-click a row.

    Expect The row’s main action (Start or Stop, for a container) and ⋮ appear at its end. Delete is in ⋮, the right-click menu and the page header, never as a button on the row, and it asks first.

  6. 42 Step through the list

    Do With a page open, use ↑ ↓ in its header, then ⌘[ and ⌘]. Then press the expand button beside them.

    Expect The selection moves through the list in the order shown. Expand widens the page over the list.

  7. 43 Several at once

    Do Tick two rows. Then hover a checkbox that can’t be ticked (This Mac in Hosts, or a built-in network).

    Expect The right side shows a summary with actions for both. A checkbox that can’t be ticked says why.

  8. 44 List only

    Do Press the list-only button (the one with lines) in the bar above the list, or press ⌥⌘L. Click a row, then press ✕ or Esc. Press the two-panes button to bring the page back.

    Expect The list fills the window, a click on a row slides its page in, and ✕ or Esc closes it, also while a row has keyboard focus. The two-panes button puts the list and the page side by side again, and the button that is lit follows.

  9. 45 Filtering keeps the page open

    Do With an item’s page open, filter the list so that item is hidden.

    Expect Its page stays open.

  10. 46 Logs and Activity

    Do Open Logs and click a line. Then open Activity and click a line.

    Expect The line’s full text, time, source and Mac on the right. Activity is a record, so it has no bulk actions.

  11. 47 Notifications and Suggestions

    Do Look at Notifications and Suggestions. Dismiss several notices at once.

    Expect The same rows as the other lists. Error notices can’t be ticked.

  12. 48 The smallest window

    Do Make the window as small as it goes.

    Expect No pane is cut off, the theme grid in Settings reflows to two columns, and the version in the status bar is still readable.

The Overview and its widgets

About 8 min · 9 steps

The Overview is the page Flotilla opens on. Under the band along the top, it is made of widgets: tiles that each show one thing, such as your containers or your Macs. You choose which ones, how big, what they show and where they go, and Flotilla remembers for next time. A few containers running first (from Suggestions) gives the widgets something to show.

  1. 49 The top of the Overview

    Do Open Overview. Look at the band along the top, under the title bar. Try to drag it or remove it.

    Expect The band says “Nothing needs attention”, or shows one notice at a time with arrows and Dismiss, and always has an All Notifications link. It stays where it is: it can’t be moved or removed. The Get started card and the widgets are under it.

  2. 50 Four shapes

    Do Find the Containers widget and click the ⋯ at its top right. Under Size, choose Small, then Wide, Tall and Large in turn.

    Expect The widget changes shape each time, and shows a different layout of the same information: a single figure when Small, the figure with a few rows when Wide, a list of one-line rows when Tall, and a list of rows with more detail when Large. Nothing overlaps and the other widgets move to make room.

  3. 51 Choose what a widget shows

    Do Open the ⋯ menu of the Containers widget again and under Show choose Running, then Stopped, then All. Do the same on Hosts and Images.

    Expect The list changes each time. The widget’s title names a choice other than its usual one, such as “Containers · Stopped”. Hosts offers All and Problems, and Images offers Newest, Largest and With Vulnerabilities. Storage has no choice, because it shows this Mac’s disk and nothing else.

  4. 52 Click a widget

    Do Set Containers to Stopped, then click the widget. Go back to Overview and click Machines.

    Expect Containers opens already filtered to stopped containers, and Machines opens its section. Clicking a widget takes you to the part of Flotilla it comes from, set to what the widget showed.

  5. 53 Add and remove widgets

    Do Press Add Widget in the bar at the top of the Overview, above the widgets. Tick a widget that has no tick, such as Images or Tasks. Then open the menu again and untick it.

    Expect Every kind of widget is listed with an icon, and those on the page have a tick. Ticking adds the widget, and unticking takes it away. There is only ever one of each kind: ticking one that is already there changes nothing.

  6. 54 Move a widget

    Do Hover over a widget and look at its top left. Drag the grip of dots to another place, and let go.

    Expect A grip of six dots appears, and its tooltip says “Drag to move”. The widget lifts as you drag, the others slide out of the way, and it settles into the place where you let go. You don’t need Edit mode for this. Clicking anywhere else on the widget opens its section instead.

  7. 55 Edit Widgets

    Do Press Edit Widgets. On one widget, press ✕. On another, press the S, W, T and L buttons, then drag the small handle at its bottom right corner. Look at the panel on the right. Then press Done.

    Expect Each widget gets a ✕, buttons for its size (S small, W wide, T tall, L large) and a resize handle. The panel lists every kind of widget with a checkbox and a line about it, and ticking or unticking works as in the Add Widget menu. At the top are Reset to Recommended and Done. Done, the panel and the sidebar all stay whole in the window, even a narrow one.

  8. 56 Your layout is kept

    Do Arrange the widgets the way you like, quit Flotilla (⌘Q) and open it again.

    Expect The same widgets, order, sizes and choices come back. The layout belongs to this Mac: another Mac that you manage keeps its own.

  9. 57 Start again

    Do Press Edit Widgets, then Reset to Recommended. Then remove every widget with ✕.

    Expect Reset puts back Fleet at a glance, Hosts, Containers, Vulnerabilities, Activity, Machines and Kubernetes at their usual sizes. With no widgets, the page says “Make the Overview yours”, with buttons to add widgets one at a time and “Use the recommended layout”. The band and the Get started card never go.

The Tasks panel

About 12 min · 15 steps

Every job you start, such as a pull or a run, becomes a task, shown in the Tasks panel on the right. A larger image, such as postgres:17, gives you time to look while it pulls.

  1. 58 A task in detail

    Do Start a larger pull. Hover its line in Tasks, then open its caret (the small arrow). Then put the panel away with its chevron or Esc.

    Expect The tooltip says where it runs, its state and step. The caret shows its steps and live output. Put away, the status bar shows “Tasks · 1 · N%” with a spinner.

  2. 59 Show and hide

    Do Press ⌥⌘T twice.

    Expect Tasks opens, then closes.

  3. 60 Two jobs are two tasks

    Do Start a second pull while the first is running.

    Expect Two lines in Tasks, and Images stays usable.

  4. 61 Stop a pull

    Do Start a larger pull and click its Stop (a circle with a square).

    Expect The task ends as Cancelled, with no failure notice.

  5. 62 A job that fails says so

    Do Pull an image that doesn’t exist, such as flotilla-beta/no-such-image:0.

    Expect The task is marked failed with one red line saying why. The status bar shows “Tasks · Failed” in red, and a notice “… failed: …” offers Show Task, which opens Tasks on that line. If Flotilla is in the background, a macOS notification too.

  6. 63 Retry a failed job

    Do On that failed task, click Retry (the circular arrow), twice.

    Expect A new line starts and the old one is marked retried. The second click starts nothing more.

  7. 64 Stop a container from its task

    Do Run a container from Suggestions, show Tasks (⌥⌘T), and hover the Stop button (a filled square) on its Run task. Click it.

    Expect The tooltip names the container, such as “Stop mailpit”. The button shows a small spinner while it works, and the container stops in Containers.

  8. 65 A run can’t be retried

    Do Look at a Run task.

    Expect No stop-circle and no Retry on it: running the same container twice could do harm, so Flotilla doesn’t offer it.

  9. 66 Stop never reaches the wrong container

    Do Run a container, delete it, run another with the same name, and look at the first one’s task.

    Expect The old task has no Stop button. The new task’s Stop stops only the new container.

  10. 67 Finished jobs fold away

    Do Let several jobs finish, one of them failing. Then use Clear on one line, and Clear finished (✕ in a circle) in the header.

    Expect They fold into one line, “N finished”, that opens on click, with the failure named on it in red (“· 1 failed”). Clear removes them.

  11. 68 A failure keeps the panel up

    Do Start two jobs, one of which fails.

    Expect The panel stays up after the good one ends, until you put it away.

  12. 69 An open line keeps the panel up

    Do Open a line’s caret, then let a job finish.

    Expect The panel does not tuck away while a line is open.

  13. 70 Tasks and Greta

    Do Open Greta (the sidebar, or ⌘G) and start a pull.

    Expect The Tasks panel and Greta don’t overlap.

  14. 71 Every icon has a tooltip

    Do Hover each icon button in the panel.

    Expect Stop, Retry, Clear and Clear finished each have a tooltip.

  15. 72 A switch for failed jobs

    Do Open Settings ▸ Notifications.

    Expect A switch for “A job you started failed”, on by default.

Forms

About 8 min · 10 steps

Its images are scanned every day for known security problems (vulnerabilities), and Flotilla matches that report against the images on your Mac; nothing about your Mac is sent.     Expect Beta 6 is offered with its release notes, installs, and relaunches Flotilla. The version now reads “1.5.0-beta.6”, not “1.5.0-beta.5”.

  2. 201 Your things are kept

    Do After the update, look at your groups, your theme and other settings, the section you had open, and any Macs you had paired under Hosts. Then open Overview.

    Expect All of it is kept. Paired Macs are still trusted, with no new pairing needed. The Overview shows the recommended widgets, because beta 5 had no widgets to keep.

  3. 202 Update a host from the admin

    Do If a host is on beta 5: on the admin, open the version menu ▸ Your Macs and update Flotilla on it.

    Expect A confirmation first. The host updates when idle, one host at a time, and comes back on beta 6.

  4. 203 Nothing left over

    Do After a host updates, open its page.

    Expect No “waiting on update” or failed notice left over. Its Helper shows Running at the new version.

  5. 204 A host offline during an update

    Do Take a host offline while it is being updated.

    Expect “Waiting”, not failed. Failed only after two minutes on the old build.

  6. 205 Mixed versions

    Do If one Mac is still on beta 5, open its page and its containers from the other.

    Expect Everything beta 5 could do still works, and nothing is refused. The two Macs keep their own Overview layouts.
